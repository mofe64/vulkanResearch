## What's inside the lamp

Orion is a small computer with senses and muscles, shaped like a lamp. A <Term id="raspberry-pi">Raspberry Pi</Term>
in the base is the brain. Microphones are its ears, a camera in the head is its eyes, a speaker is its voice, five
motors are its muscles, and a ring of LEDs is both its lamp and its way of signalling.

<Fact>Every part is something you can buy; the full list is on the Resources tab.</Fact>

## One sentence, end to end

Ask Orion a question and it passes through a chain of programs, each doing one job and handing its result to the
next: hear the <Term id="wake-word">wake word</Term>, turn speech into text, write a reply, turn the reply into a voice,
then speak and move.

Most of the chain runs on the lamp. Only the reply itself is written by an online <Term id="language-model">language model</Term>.

<Fact>[How does Orion hold conversations?](/projects/orion/learn/how-orion-holds-conversations/) follows this chain step by step.</Fact>

<UnderTheHood>
The programs are separate services on the Pi: `orion-listener` (microphone and wake word), `orion-voice-stack`
(coordinator, Qwen and Piper workers, and the agent that talks to Codex), `orion-vision` (camera) and `oriond`
(motors, light and speaker). They talk over local sockets. See `docs/system-architecture.md`.
</UnderTheHood>

## Only one part moves the motors

Many parts of Orion have reasons to move it: voice wants it to turn toward you, the camera wants to follow your hand,
Studio wants to play a scene. None of them drive the motors. They all ask one program, the
<Term id="runtime">runtime</Term>, which checks every request first.

Having one gatekeeper is a safety rule. It means one place decides about limits, rest and Stop, and nothing can sneak
around it.

## Fast loop, slow thinking

The runtime steers the motors 50 times a second. Recognising speech or writing a reply takes seconds. If the motors
had to wait for that thinking, Orion would freeze mid-gesture every time it listened.

So the slow work runs in separate programs. The fast loop never waits for them; it just picks up their results when
they're ready.

<Fact>Model inference and network waits always run outside the 50-a-second movement loop. [How does Orion move?](/projects/orion/learn/how-orion-moves/) shows that loop up close.</Fact>

## What needs the internet

Orion does most of its work on the lamp: hearing, understanding speech, speaking, moving, the light, alarms and the
camera. Only two things go online: the <Term id="language-model">language model</Term> that writes its replies, and
web search.

That keeps it quick and private. Your voice is turned into text on the lamp; what goes online is the text.

<Fact>Camera images stay in the Pi's memory. One goes online when you ask Orion to look at something, and with each spoken remark in Demo mode. More in [How does Orion see?](/projects/orion/learn/how-orion-sees/).</Fact>

## Awake, resting, waking

When nobody has talked to it for a while, Orion folds down and switches its motors off. Saying "Hey Orion" brings it
back. In between, it's always in exactly one of a few named states, and only certain events can move it from one to
the next.

Describing it as a <Term id="state-machine">state machine</Term> is what makes it dependable: there's no state where
it's half asleep.

<UnderTheHood>
Status reports `disabled`, `awake`, `going_to_rest`, `resting`, `waking` or `fault`. Torque is released only after the
motors measure that the rest pose was reached. A failed sleep step tries a direct rest once; if that also fails, Orion
returns home and keeps its light on. See "Automatic rest and waking" in `docs/system-architecture.md`.
</UnderTheHood>

## Studio

Studio is a desktop app for controlling Orion from your computer. It pairs with the lamp over your home network, and
lets you move it, edit poses and scenes, change settings and watch what it's doing.

Studio is a remote control, not the brain. Closing it leaves Orion running exactly as before.

<Fact>Studio reaches the lamp through one authenticated gateway, which only accepts a fixed list of requests.</Fact>
