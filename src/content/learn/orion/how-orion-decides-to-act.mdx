<p class="intro">
So far Orion has mostly responded: to its name, to a face, to a hand. This lesson is about what it does on its own
initiative: glancing at something new, saying hello when you sit down, suggesting a break. Doing that well is mostly a
matter of knowing when not to.
</p>

<Section>

## One picture of the moment

Many parts of Orion could decide to act, and if each asked its own questions they would soon disagree. So every time the
camera reports, Orion builds one <Term id="situation">situation</Term>: who is in view, whether anyone is looking at it,
how long they've been here, the time, the light, and whether it's busy.

Every initiative then takes the same path: a reaction, a check against the curiosity budget, the movement, and only
then, maybe, words.

<Fact>A visit starts when a face comes into view and ends when nobody has been seen for 10 minutes.</Fact>

<UnderTheHood>
The situation is built in `runtime/src/expression/situation.rs`; the shared path is in `runtime/src/expression/initiative.rs`.
Each decision is written to the behaviour log with the situation at that moment, including the things Orion chose not
to do and why, which Studio's Debug page shows.
</UnderTheHood>

## Quiet, Lively or Demo

How much Orion does on its own is one setting in Studio. Quiet reacts with movement only. Lively can also speak first
and looks after the people at the desk. Demo turns everything up, for showing Orion to a room of people.

<Fact>Demo also sends one camera picture with each remark, so the reply can mention what Orion sees.</Fact>

## Every lamp is a little different

When a new Orion first starts, it picks two <Term id="quirk">quirks</Term> at random: how curious it is and how chatty
it is. A nosy lamp glances at new things often; a shy one rarely. A chatty lamp speaks up more and may add a playful
thought; a reserved one keeps it brief.

The same numbers that limit curiosity and speech come from the quirks, so the difference is real, not cosmetic.

<Fact>The quirks are saved on the lamp, and Studio shows them under Personality and memory.</Fact>

## Speaking first

A Lively Orion can say a short <Term id="remark">remark</Term> on its own: a hello when you arrive, a comment when you
hold its gaze, a word about something new on the desk. A lamp that chatted constantly would be unbearable, so every
remark has to pass a list of rules first.

It speaks to each person once per visit, keeps remarks apart, says nothing at night, after a conversation, or while
you're concentrating, and waits longer each time a remark is ignored.

<Predict question="Orion mentions your new umbrella and you don't answer. How long until its next remark?" options={["After 10 minutes", "After 20 minutes"]} answer={1}>
At least 20 minutes, for a normal lamp. An ignored remark doubles the usual 10-minute gap (up to four times), and a reply resets it.
</Predict>

<UnderTheHood>
`runtime/src/expression/remarks.rs` checks the rules in this order: busy, asked to be quiet, already spoken this visit,
concentrating, quiet hours, within 5 minutes of a conversation, spacing, the cooldown for that kind of remark (30 min
for arrivals, 20 for looks, an hour for new things) and the hourly limit. The words come from the language model, which
can't use tools for a remark and can choose to say nothing.
</UnderTheHood>

## How a remark is said

The movement always comes first: Orion turns to you, then speaks. The language model writes the remark from what just
happened and Orion's recent remarks, in the style its quirks call for. Then Orion listens, so you can answer without
saying "Hey Orion".

You stay in charge. Saying "Hey Orion" during a remark stops it, and "be quiet for a while" silences remarks for up to
8 hours.

<Fact>A remark that can't start within 8 seconds of its moment is dropped: old news isn't worth saying.</Fact>
</Section>

<Section>

## Waving back

Raise your hand to Orion near your face, and a Lively lamp waves back: the head turns to you, the arm lifts and sways
side to side twice, and it nods. No words, just the gesture.

<Fact>The hand has to be up near a face for 0.6 seconds, so reaching for a mug doesn't count. Waving back uses the curiosity budget too.</Fact>

## Looking after you

A Lively Orion also keeps an eye on the person at the desk. After a long stretch of work it stretches and suggests a
break. When the room gets dark and its own light is low, it offers to turn its light on. A late goodbye becomes a
goodnight, and Orion heads to rest soon after.

<Fact>Each of these happens only while someone is in view, and the break and light offers at most once a visit.</Fact>

## A memory of home

Orion remembers a little about the home it lives in. When you mention something coming up, such as an interview or a
trip, it saves a short note and may ask how it went on the day or just after. It also notices habits, like when you
usually arrive, and keeps one sentence about each of the last 30 days.

This memory is background for conversations and welcomes, never something Orion brings up when nobody is there.

<Fact>Home memory is stored on the lamp. Studio shows every part of it and can delete any item, or all of it.</Fact>

<Section>

## You set the limits

Everything in this lesson is adjustable. Owners can pick Quiet, Lively or Demo, move quiet hours, change
how often remarks may come, and switch off any single behaviour. And Orion writes down each decision with its reason, so
when it stays quiet, you can see why.

<Fact>Nothing Orion notices can wake it or switch its motors on, and apart from a goodnight it never speaks first during quiet hours.</Fact>
